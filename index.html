Field report · OpenAI · Daybreak · Persona

Two hardware keys in. No document that counts.

I set up everything OpenAI Daybreak asks for before identity verification, including two YubiKeys from OpenAI's own Yubico promotion. Then Persona rejected my country of citizenship. When I chose my country of residence instead, it offered only documents I can't hold. OpenAI's support assistant says Daybreak verification "can include residence permits". The flow offers none.

What Persona shows

Russia: “We're unable to verify IDs from this country.”

Japan: driver's license or passport only. There is no option for the Japanese Residence Card (在留カード), the government photo ID every mid- to long-term foreign resident carries.

The awkward part

OpenAI's own support assistant told me Daybreak document types “can include residence permits.” The only residence permit I could possibly use is the one Japan issues to every foreign resident, and Persona's Japan flow doesn't list it.

If Daybreak is closed to Russian citizens wherever they live, that may be a legitimate legal choice for a trusted-access program around cyber-capable models. But then it should be written on the eligibility page before anyone buys hardware keys, not discovered at the last step.

What OpenAI needs to answer

  1. Is individual Daybreak access unavailable to Russian citizens who live in a supported country? If so, cite the rule.
  2. Show any nationality or region limit on the eligibility page before hardware-key and Advanced Account Security setup.
  3. If nationality is not disqualifying, why does the Japan path omit the Japanese Residence Card?
  4. Provide a supported alternative: a residence-permit path, manual document review, or organization-level verification.

Not a demand for automatic access

Trusted-access programs may have legitimate eligibility limits. This report asks OpenAI to state its limit plainly, before users invest in the setup, and to reconcile its support guidance with what the verification flow actually offers.
